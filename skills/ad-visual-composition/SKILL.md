# Ad visual composition

Use this skill when creating or revising an editable social ad. Optimize for one fast reading path, not decorative density.

## Establish the hierarchy

Rank the information: hook first, proof or mechanism second, offer third, action last. If two messages compete for first place, cut or subordinate one. Keep claims accurate and supplied by the user; never invent metrics, reviews, guarantees, scarcity, or endorsements.

## Lay out the canvas

- Preserve a safety margin of roughly 6–10% on all sides.
- Use one dominant visual or typographic block.
- Create contrast by scale, weight, spacing, and surface before adding more colors.
- Keep body copy readable at the intended mobile display size.
- Give text a quiet area or a deliberate solid surface; do not place it over busy detail.
- Use alignment and repeated edges to make separate layers feel intentional.
- Prefer 3–6 purposeful layers to a pile of small decorations.

## Work as editable layers

Keep the hero image, background surfaces, hook, proof, offer, and CTA separately editable. After tool calls, inspect the design record for bounds, overlap, reading order, contrast, and whether every agent-editable property can also be changed in the visible inspector.

Example for a 1080 × 1080 offer ad: place a quiet hero image across the lower 52%, a high-contrast hook in the upper-left within an 80 px margin, a one-line proof point below it, and a compact offer/CTA surface near the lower-left. Make the hook roughly twice the proof text size and ensure neither crosses the image's focal subject.
